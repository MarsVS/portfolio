When Every Second Counts

Medical emergencies are unpredictable and stressful. Our trauma room and intensive care unit (ICU) are continuously equipped with advanced monitoring equipment, oxygen therapy, instant point-of-care diagnostics, and experienced veterinary critical care staff ready to stabilize your pet immediately.

  • Immediate clinical triage with priority access for critical cases
  • On-site emergency surgery, trauma resuscitation, and blood transfusions
  • In-house FAST ultrasound, digital X-rays, and stat laboratory panels

Critical Symptoms Requiring Immediate Care

If your pet exhibits any of the following symptoms, bring them to our emergency facility immediately:

Difficulty breathing, severe panting, or pale/blue gums
Acute abdominal swelling, unproductive retching (GDV/Bloat)
Inability to urinate or extreme distress while straining
Seizures lasting over 2 minutes or multiple cluster seizures
Suspected toxin ingestion, severe trauma, or active arterial bleeding

Action Plan

What to Do in a Pet Emergency

Follow these key steps to prepare your pet for safe, rapid emergency treatment.

1

Call Ahead Immediately

Notify our emergency desk while en route so our surgical and critical care team can prepare oxygen, medications, and the trauma suite for your arrival.

2

Safe Transport

Keep your pet calm and warm. Wrap injured pets in a blanket to prevent shock, keep the spine stable if trauma is suspected, and do not muzzle if breathing is impaired.

3

Immediate Triage

Upon arrival, our veterinary staff will evaluate vital signs. Patients with life-threatening conditions are taken directly to treatment for immediate stabilization.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an appointment for emergency care?

No appointment is ever required for an emergency. Walk-ins are welcomed, but calling ahead allows us to prepare life-saving equipment before you walk through the door.

How does triage work in a veterinary emergency room?

Just like a human hospital emergency room, patients are prioritized based on medical severity. Critical, life-threatening cases (such as cardiac arrest, respiratory failure, or severe bleeding) receive immediate attention before stable conditions.

What should I bring with me to the emergency clinic?

If possible, bring any packaging or containers of toxins/medications your pet may have ingested, a list of current prescription drugs, medical records if available, and a leash or secure pet carrier.
