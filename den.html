Oral Health & Systemic Wellness

Healthy Mouth, Healthy Pet

By age three, over 80% of dogs and cats show signs of periodontal disease. Dental bacteria doesn't just cause foul breath and tooth loss—it can enter the bloodstream and damage vital organs like the heart, liver, and kidneys. Routine veterinary dental cleaning is essential to long-term health.

  • Ultrasonic scaling above and below the gumline to remove calculus
  • Digital dental X-rays to detect hidden tooth root disease and bone loss
  • Polishing, fluoride treatments, and custom home oral care plans

Signs of Dental Disease

Contact us if you notice any of these common symptoms in your dog or cat:

Persistent bad breath (halitosis)
Yellow or brown tartar build-up on teeth
Red, swollen, or bleeding gums
Difficulty chewing, dropping food, or loss of appetite
Loose, broken, or missing teeth

Comprehensive Dental Procedures

Complete oral healthcare performed under safe, monitored anesthesia.

Ultrasonic Cleaning

Professional scaling to strip away hardened plaque and calculus from all enamel surfaces.

What Happens During a Dental Procedure?

A step-by-step overview of your pet's dental cleaning day.

1

Pre-Dental Assessment

Pre-anesthetic blood testing ensures internal organs can safely clear medications. We place an IV catheter for fluids and precise drug delivery.

2

Cleaning & Treatment

Under general anesthesia with an endotracheal tube protecting the airway, we take full dental X-rays, scale, probe, polish, and extract diseased teeth if necessary.

3

Home Care Strategy

After smooth recovery, we review dental photos/X-rays with you and send you home with personalized toothbrushing guidance or VOHC-approved dental diets.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my pet need general anesthesia for a dental cleaning?

Anesthesia is required to clean beneath the gumline where 80% of bacteria live, take accurate intraoral X-rays, and protect the pet's airway from water mist and debris. Non-anesthetic dental cleanings are purely cosmetic and cannot treat underlying periodontal disease.

How often should my pet get professional dental cleanings?

Most dogs and cats benefit from annual professional dental cleanings starting around age 2 to 3. Small breed dogs and pets prone to heavy tartar buildup may require dental evaluation every 6 to 12 months.

How can I maintain my pet's teeth at home?

Daily brushing using pet-safe toothpaste (never human toothpaste) is the gold standard. Additionally, dental chews, water additives, and prescription dental diets approved by the Veterinary Oral Health Council (VOHC) help reduce plaque formation.
